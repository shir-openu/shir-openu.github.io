Example 2 — The Plane \( f(x,y) = y - x \)

A plane and its level lines

Given the ODE

\( y' = y - x, \)

the surface \( f(x,y) = y - x \) describes a plane.

The level curves of this plane are the lines

\( y - x = C \;\Rightarrow\; y = x + C. \)

Vectors of constant slope on each isocline

On each isocline of height \( C \) we draw vectors whose slope is \( C \):

For \( C = 2 \) we get \( y = x + 2 \). On this line we draw vectors with direction \( \arctan(2) \approx 63^\circ \).

For \( C = 1 \) we get \( y = x + 1 \). On this line we draw vectors with direction \( \arctan(1) = 45^\circ \).

For \( C = 0 \) we get \( y = x \). On this line we draw vectors with direction \( \arctan(0) = 0^\circ \).

For \( C = -1 \) we get \( y = x - 1 \). On this line we draw vectors with direction \( \arctan(-1) = -45^\circ \).

The dense direction field and three solutions

Continuing this way we obtain the dense direction field. Note that the vectors of the same isocline all point in the same direction. That direction is \( \arctan C \), where \( C \) is the height of the isocline on the surface \( f(x,y) = y - x \).

Every solution of the ODE must advance so that it is always tangent to the direction field. The panel shows the solutions obtained for

\( y(0) = 0, \qquad y(0) = 2, \qquad y(2) = 1. \)
