Example 3 — The Surface \( f(x,y) = (1-xy)^3 \)

The surface

Given the ODE

\( y' = (1 - xy)^3, \)

we sketch the corresponding surface

\( f(x,y) = (1 - xy)^3. \)

On each isocline of height \( C \),

\( (1 - xy)^3 = C, \)

we draw vectors whose slope is \( C \).

Vectors on each isocline

For \( C = -8 \): on the curve \( (1-xy)^3 = -8 \) we get \( xy = 3 \), and we draw on the curve vectors with direction \( \theta = \arctan(-8) \approx -83^\circ \).

For \( C = -1 \): on the curve \( (1-xy)^3 = -1 \) we get \( xy = 2 \), and we draw on the curve vectors with direction \( \theta = \arctan(-1) = -45^\circ \).

For \( C = 0 \): on the curve \( (1-xy)^3 = 0 \) we get \( xy = 1 \), and we draw on the curve vectors with direction \( \theta = \arctan(0) = 0^\circ \).

For \( C = 1 \): on the curve \( (1-xy)^3 = 1 \) we get \( xy = 0 \), and the field vector makes the angle \( \theta = \arctan(1) = 45^\circ \).

The dense direction field and five solutions

Continuing this way we obtain the dense direction field. Note that the vectors of the same isocline all point in the same direction — that direction is \( \arctan C \), where \( C \) is the height of the isocline on the surface \( f(x,y) = (1-xy)^3 \).

The solutions of the equation must be tangent to the dense direction field. The panel shows five solutions of the equation for the initial conditions:

\( (-2,\, 1.5), \quad (0,\, 0), \quad (2,\, -1.2), \quad (1,\, 1.5), \quad (0.5,\, 2.5). \)
