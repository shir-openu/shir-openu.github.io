Stitching Solutions

Where the uniqueness conditions fail, several solutions may share a point. Two of them can then be stitched at a junction \( x_0 \) as long as value and slope match:

\( y_1(x_0)=y_2(x_0), \qquad y_1'(x_0)=y_2'(x_0). \)

The join is then continuous and differentiable, so the stitched curve is itself a solution.

Example

For

\( y' = 2 e^{x}\sqrt{|y|} \)

separation gives three solutions through \( (0,0) \):

\( y=(e^{x}-1)^2,\; y=-(e^{x}-1)^2,\; y\equiv 0. \)

Since \( \sqrt{|y|} \) is not Lipschitz at \( y=0 \), uniqueness fails there. So we may run along \( y=0 \) and branch off at \( x_0=0 \), where both value and slope are \( 0 \).
