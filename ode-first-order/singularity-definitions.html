Singular Solutions

A singular point of \( y'=f(x,y) \) is a point where the existence-and-uniqueness conditions fail. A singular solution is a solution passing entirely through such points — so through each of its points more than one solution runs.

Typically a singular solution is the envelope of the general-solution family: it touches every member and is not itself one of them. Uniqueness fails all along it, because at each point it and one family curve share the same value and slope.

How to spot one
Look for a curve tangent to the whole family. It solves the ODE, yet no value of the constant in the general solution produces it.
