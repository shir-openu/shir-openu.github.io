Existence Domain vs Solution Domain

Let \( D \) be the region where the existence-and-uniqueness conditions hold, and \( D' \) the domain on which an actual solution lives. These two need not coincide.

Solutions can extend past \( D \)

For

\( y' - \dfrac{y}{x} = x \)

the right-hand side is undefined on \( x=0 \), so \( D=\{x\neq 0\} \). Yet the solutions

\( y = x^{2} + C x \)

are smooth on all of \( \mathbb{R} \) and pass through the origin: here \( D \subset D' \).

Solutions can break down inside \( D \)

For

\( y' = y^{2} \)

both \( f \) and \( f_y \) are continuous everywhere, so \( D=\mathbb{R}^2 \). But the solution through \( (0,1) \),

\( y = -\dfrac{1}{x-1}, \)

blows up at \( x=1 \): its domain is only \( (-\infty, 1) \), so \( D' \subset D \).
